BUYER QUESTIONS

The questions
behind the decision.

Get the details clear before committing to a rollout.

Security and deploymentPricing and scope
Is portfolio performance available today?

It is a planned workflow. This example helps define what the view should answer. Confirm the current capability, data preparation, integration work, and development commitments for the scope you need.

What would we compare across sites?

Start with decisions you make today: open reviews, unresolved findings, overdue work, rework, or customer demand. Define the metric and reporting period, then prove that the underlying records support the summary.

How would missing or stale data be handled?

Make coverage and freshness visible. The example leaves a site’s demand unavailable instead of treating it as zero. Agree the acceptable update rate and who resolves gaps for each source.

Can we drill into the issue behind a site’s score?

The direction is to connect the summary to source records and local context. Start by defining which records a leader needs to inspect before assigning an owner or changing a resource plan.

Can this identify upsell opportunities?

Customer demand approaching a contractual limit can be a reason for an account conversation. It does not prove physical capacity or that the customer will buy more. Review the demand history, customer plans, and engineering constraints together.

How do we compare a new site with a mature site?

Keep the commissioning stage, workload mix, customer restrictions, and data coverage visible. Use like-for-like definitions and relevant peer groups rather than a single unexplained ranking.
