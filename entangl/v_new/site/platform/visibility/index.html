WHAT CHANGES FOR YOUR TEAM

Replace the status chase
with a record people can inspect.

A useful shared view starts with the question the customer or auditor is trying to answer.

Make the status understandable

Distinguish draft, review, open findings, required approvals, work in progress, and completed evidence.

Share within an agreed scope

Define who can see a provider, site, hall, asset, procedure, or record. Test the boundaries from the recipient’s perspective.

Keep evidence connected

Relate the source, procedure version, decision, execution evidence, and customer update so the history can be followed.

THE DETAILS THAT MATTER

Answer the question
behind the request.

Different stakeholders need different levels of detail from the same operating history.

Discuss systems and integrations
01

For the customer

What work affects our service? Has it been reviewed? What needs our approval? What update are we allowed to share internally?

02

For the operator

What remains open? Which version is in use? Who owns the decision? Has the customer-facing update been approved for publication?

03

For the auditor

Which source and procedure version were used? Who approved the work? What evidence supports completion? What exceptions remain unresolved?

04

For an insurer or acquiring owner

What does the operating record demonstrate about maintenance, incident response, open findings, and follow-through? Define the appropriate evidence scope.

BUYER QUESTIONS

The questions
behind the decision.

Get the details clear before committing to a rollout.

Security and deploymentPricing and scope
Can customers see the status without asking us for another email?

That is the shared-view direction. Define the procedure states, updates, source information, and audience access included in your deployment. Live progress depends on the underlying workflow and integrations.

Can we restrict the view to one customer’s hall or equipment?

Those boundaries belong in the access design. Test each role against permitted and excluded sites, halls, assets, and records, including what happens when a person’s responsibility changes.

Will an AI review replace the customer’s own approval?

A review record can support the customer’s decision. The customer still determines which checks and approvals its process requires. Make software findings and final acceptance clearly distinguishable.

Can the auditor see the source and the version that was used?

Use that as an evaluation requirement. Demonstrate the relationship between the source, procedure version, decision, and completion evidence, including how later revisions are represented.

What gets published automatically?

Define publication rules explicitly. A useful starting scope separates the internal working record from the customer-facing update and identifies who can approve release.

Can this support insurance or acquisition diligence?

A structured operating history can be useful evidence in those discussions. Agree the audience and records in scope. It does not itself establish compliance, insurability, a premium discount, or acceptance by an acquiring owner.

WORKFLOW SCOPE

Shared visibility is scoped with your deployment. Define site, equipment, customer, and role boundaries together with publication and approval responsibilities.
